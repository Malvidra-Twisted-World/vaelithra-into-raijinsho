
### Scene: The Hollow of Warm Breath

**Setting**
The path through Raijinsho’s snowfield winds between black stones that jut like broken teeth.
The storm has dulled to a slow drift when the party feels a faint current of warmth — the kind that shouldn’t exist here.
Following it, they come upon a hollow in the ice: a shallow cave where the snow refuses to settle.
A small fire burns inside, blue-orange and steady, feeding on a fuel that doesn’t smoke.

**What they see**
A lone man crouches beside the flame. His cloak is ragged but mended with meticulous care, and a line of frost traces the curve of his horns before fading into heat shimmer. A travel pack lies neatly arranged: dried meat, vials of ink, a single knife kept razor clean.
When he hears them approach, he doesn’t reach for the blade. He only exhales, as if he’s been expecting the interruption for years.

> “You can put your weapons away,” he says without turning.
> “If I wanted you dead, the snow would have swallowed you already.”

**Who he is**
Azriel looks nothing like a demon in hiding should. His features are handsome but worn, the faint red of his skin dulled by frost. His eyes burn low — not bright infernal flame, more like coals that refuse to die.
He speaks with precision, a voice that knows too many tongues. There’s a faint smell of sulfur under the cold air.

**Tone of the scene**
Quiet, tense, almost intimate. He’s not hostile; he’s testing. Every word measures how much they know.
He recognizes uniforms, crests, even minor holy symbols; anyone wearing marks of Baldur’s Gate or infernal orders catches his eye.
If the Warlock bears their insignia, he notes it immediately and smiles without warmth.

> “So they finally sent another. You wear the colors better than the last one. He screamed until the cold took him.”

**Interaction hooks**

* He can offer information: a shortcut to the dragon’s lair, the nature of the frost, the way to survive the caves.
* He won’t bargain with lies; he only trades truth for truth.
* If the Warlock insists on arrest or violence, he fights like a cornered animal—fast, vicious, but more about escape than victory.

**If spared or captured**

* Azriel can be marched back to the ship after the island’s climax.
* If he survives the journey, the frost seems weaker around him — as if his presence disturbs the island’s magic.
* He gives one last line before boarding Thania’s vessel:

  > “Tell your masters I kept their secret. The frost never reached Baldur’s Gate because I made it stop here.”
